Learning Distributed Coloring Rules:
Functional Success, Continuations, and Transfer
Research collaboration
31 October 2026
Abstract
What does successful execution reveal about a learned distributed algorithm? We study local neural
decision modules inside explicit coloring interpreters. Seven scoped teachers and three architectures
yield 21 functionally passing combinations, each with five checkpoints and 1,000 successful runs per
checkpoint under heterogeneous protocols. Frozen transfer/control conditions give 449,791 successful
runs out of 450,000 in the primary cohort, with failures in two checkpoint–condition cells. Against this
positive functional context, we define typed continuation contracts and show how perfect measured native
agreement can leave responses to alternative messages undetermined. Separate paired studies expose the
role of acquired support: counterfactual supervision improves some continuation measures while sometimes
reducing autonomous completion. We distinguish occurrence, input-type and execution-relevant errors
rather than treating one aggregate accuracy as algorithm identification. Formal transition statements and
component interventions rescue 1,799 selected CF failures by replacing COMMIT alone and a different
796 failures by replacing participation alone; transfer replays distinguish unsafe acceptances from correct
vetoes that leave progress or scheduled repair unresolved. A neighbor-message follow-up provides a
separate positive example of correcting a context-coverage defect. The contribution is a scoped evaluation
of learned local rules and their failure mechanisms, not a universal correctness or training-superiority
claim.
1 Introduction
A neural policy can produce valid colorings without reproducing every decision rule of its teacher. Conversely,
a policy can reject a bad proposal correctly and still fail to finish. We study these distinctions in a setting
where learned local modules are combined with declared communication, decoding and scheduling interfaces.
The policies work broadly on finite benchmarks: all seven teacher variants have passing packages for each of
three architectures, and sixteen of eighteen extensible combinations pass all five selected transfer conditions
in all five training seeds.
Functional success is therefore the starting point for asking what was learned. In one comparison, three
BE/E1 procedures match every measured native action but respond differently when a participation message
or queried option changes. The off-native reference is a specified continuation contract, not an intention
uniquely recoverable from a successful trace. In another comparison, equal-type continuation accuracy
improves in all ten BE seed pairs while autonomous success decreases. The errors have different consequences:
a confirmation at a silent root cannot write, whereas rejecting a mandatory KW recoloring violates the next
bound. Under frozen transfer, two adjacent vertices can repeatedly propose the same color and correctly veto
one another; a speculative repair can likewise be correctly vetoed without resolving the existing conflict.
Our contribution is an empirical study of these relations with three connected parts. First, we give scoped
teacher interfaces, explicit learned and supplied modules, native functional coverage and frozen transfer.
Second, we define continuation contracts and compare supervised procedures with class, support and type
accounting, retaining uncertain and adverse outcomes. Third, we connect continuation errors to execution
through precise transition statements, conditional progress arguments and replayable component interventions.
The resulting question is which acquired local responses matter on reached executions, rather than whether
one global accuracy identifies an algorithm. Elementary support arguments clarify the question; they are not
claimed as new general learning lower bounds.

Relation to previous work. Neural graph coloring predates this study (Lemos et al., 2019), as do graph￾algorithm execution with MAX aggregation (Veličković et al., 2020) and trace-based reasoning benchmarks
(Veličković et al., 2022) within neural algorithmic reasoning (Veličković and Blundell, 2021). DAgger addresses
learner-induced state distributions (Ross et al., 2011); causal confusion in imitation already motivates
interventions (de Haan et al., 2019). Our fixed-bank teacher continuations are not a new formulation
of these general ideas. GNN/local-computation connections and information restrictions are established
(Loukas, 2020; Sato et al., 2019). SALSA-CLRS already includes distributed and randomized algorithms
(Minder et al., 2023). Alignment and schedules affect extrapolation (Xu et al., 2020, 2021; Mathys et al.,
2023); asynchronous robustness requires its own assumptions (Solodova et al., 2025), which we do not test.
Recent coloring methods learn parallel orderings (Langedal and Manne, 2025), use recursive warm starts
(Vanderbush and Weber, 2026), or learn contrastive coloring geometry with transfer (Le et al., 2026). Recent
sufficient conditions for provable algorithm learning (Wittig et al., 2026) go beyond our finite extrapolation
evidence. Exact local-instruction learning under bounded-degree and finite-precision assumptions is studied
by Qharabagh et al. (2026); discrete neural reasoning already uses finite transition verification (Rodionov and
Prokhorenkova, 2025). Our conditional MAX certificate and finite transfer tests do not establish those general
guarantees. Hint-ReLIC already regularizes algorithmic predictions across input augmentations preserving
the next trace step (Bevilacqua et al., 2023); we instead acquire explicit teacher-continuation labels under
alternative messages, whose targets can change. Interchange-intervention training and causal distillation
already match high-level counterfactual computations (Geiger et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2022); our alignment is
fixed at a typed message interface and is not a new causal-training paradigm. No-hint reasoning already
separates terminal learning from trajectory imitation (Rodionov and Prokhorenkova, 2023). Mechanistic gaps
between effective and faithful execution are studied in Saldyt and Kambhampati (2025); concurrent work
finds failures despite fitting every training hint (Najafgholizadeh and Afsharchi, 2026). Our specific increment
is the joint accounting of contract, acquired support, reached error strata and safety/progress mechanisms for
these distributed coloring modules. Appendix K compares candidate contributions with precedents.
2 Interfaces and Continuation Targets
We use finite simple undirected labeled graphs G = (V, E), n = |V |, d(v) = |N(v)| and maximum degree ∆.
Native training uses ∆ ≤ 3 and Cv = {0, 1, 2, 3}. Transfer uses either the global list Cv = {0, . . . , ∆} or local
initial segments Cv = {0, . . . , d(v)}. These are not arbitrary list-coloring instances. Partial colors satisfy
xv ∈ {−1} ∪ Cv, where −1 is pending. Availability is Av = Cv \ {xu : u ∈ N(v), xu ≠ −1}. For a pending
vertex, Uv = N(v) ∩ {u : xu = −1} and dU (v) = |Uv|.
A teacher is a specified state machine with observations, private inputs, phases and an interpreter. The
learned policy receives declared observations and emits decisions; the interpreter applies them. Anonymous
trials use two neighbor exchanges; ColorTrials communicates availability-dependent preferences in the second.
JP and Greedy need one exchange mathematically, but their neural decomposition retains two. GM has
supplied blocks, barriers and a serial repair queue; BE supplies algebraic predicates and a reduction clock.
These are distinct computational regimes, not equal-cost anonymous LOCAL algorithms. Message length
and palette computation grow with palette size; we make no CONGEST complexity claim. Recorded native
steps count state-machine applications. BE has two point-reduction steps and thirteen KW steps; neural
point decisions use the first exchange, whereas KW evaluates the second stage as well. Fifteen native steps
are not a fifteen-round learned communication complexity bound.
Mathematical random statements use fresh independent ideal draws. Implementation uses namespaced
PCG64 streams and float32 observations. Required IDs are fixed distinct integers in {0, . . . , 65535}. The
implementation restricts 1 ≤ n ≤ 65536, including anonymous experiments for convenience; this is not an
anonymous-algorithm size lower bound.
Functional success, continuation fidelity and transfer. For an input/tape distribution D, interpreter
I and horizon H, let Fπ(D; I, H) be the probability of a complete proper in-list coloring within H, satisfying
the protocol’s checked invariants. A cutoff is a benchmark failure, not proof of nontermination. For an
admissible rooted query distribution Q, let Cπ,T (Q) = EQ1[gπ(S, p, c) = OT (S, p, c)], where gπ is the queried
confirmation module. Class balancing and equal-type weighting define different Q weights and are reported
2
separately. Transfer asks whether functional success or continuation fidelity persists under a specified change in
D or Q, with fixed learned tensors and any declared input adapter. It is an evaluation axis for either property,
not a third independent output criterion. Action fidelity compares common states/private inputs. Transition
fidelity compares updated states; different action codes need not yield different transitions. Continuation
fidelity evaluates a declared oracle OT (S, a) after a typed message or color-query intervention. Such messages
need not be sampled by the native teacher. Execution costs use teacher-specific units. Distributional fidelity
concerns the full terminal vector and status on a fixed labeled graph. Terminal validity alone does not imply
any of the other properties.

6.1 A prospective input-context comparison
We test a separate acquisition-strategy contrast with BE/E1 and GM/E1 (19,396 parameters). Ten fresh
paired seeds per teacher give forty models. Before training, a fixed SHA256 partition holds out 20% of
sufficient queried-slot COMMIT type buckets, common across seeds. Phase, root/context features and distinct
neighbor message types determine this key; MAX removes multiplicity. Neither training arm supervises a

KW lowers the bound to q regardless of whether a vertex wrote. An unchanged av ≥ q therefore violates the
new bound, independently of neighbor writes. The benchmark checks that invariant after each transition and
stops on violation.
Thus a false acceptance at a silent root has no direct write effect, whereas a false rejection of an eligible
active free KW option is immediately consequential. An ineligible active acceptance can change execution if
an upstream participation error makes it reachable and decoding changes the stored value; eligibility alone is
not an interpreter shield. These are fixed-state statements, not claims that every off-policy error is harmless
or every local error causes failure in every protocol.

GM repair is a suffix property. Correct marks covering every speculative conflict edge, followed by a
currently free write at every queued vertex, are sufficient for proper completion. For any initially conflicting
edge, the last repaired endpoint avoids the other; an edge with no repaired endpoint was already proper.
These conditions are not individually necessary. If a failed repair leaves an edge monochromatic and neither
endpoint changes color later, that edge remains a final conflict. If another queued endpoint later writes
a free color, it may resolve the edge. For example, three one-vertex blocks on a path with increasing IDs
1, 2, 3 simultaneously assign color 0. DETECT marks vertices 1 and 2. Skipping repair 1 and repairing 2 to
color 1 still gives (0, 1, 0). This is a legal execution of the supplied schedule. A repair veto must therefore be
interpreted with the remaining queue, rather than treated as a universal implication of failure.

9 Discussion and Limits
Functional success, fidelity to a specified continuation and transfer answer different questions. The positive
coverage establishes that these local policies can execute heterogeneous coloring procedures over substantial
finite domains. It does not identify how a particular module responds to every alternative input. Transfer
evaluates whether either property persists under a declared domain shift; good functional transfer does not
imply continuation fidelity on unvisited queries.
The paired acquisition studies show that support and weighting determine what a continuation score
measures. Native GM confirmations contain no negative color labels. BE’s dominant negative type makes an
occupancy-blind eligibility rule strong by occurrence BA, while equal-type accuracy rewards rarer decisions.
Equal target counts do not equalize information. Nearly all coarse oracle configurations occur in CF training,
despite disjoint fine input types. Neither new-logical-condition extrapolation nor an advantage over other
informative-negative acquisitions is established. The wide BE seed-paired interval leaves its mean occurrence
advantage uncertain; it does not establish a zero effect. These limits constrain the estimand, rather than
invalidating every measured acquisition difference.
Transition relevance explains why these scores need not track completion. A silent root’s confirmation
cannot write; an active acceptance can also be inert when decoding preserves its old value. In contrast,
missing a mandatory shrinking-bound write immediately breaks a KW invariant. The 30,000 fixed-component
executions localize all selected CF failures: COMMIT-only replacements rescue 1,799, and participation-only
replacements rescue the other 796. This demonstrates sufficiency of the respective component corrections
on consumed cases, not a learned remedy or a general causal relation between fidelity and success. The
new neighbor-context follow-up supplies a separate positive training intervention; it does not address every
possible BE failure mechanism.
Johansson and GM show different limits of correct rejection. A veto cannot supply proposal diversity, and
a repair veto must be judged against the remaining queue. A later endpoint may clear a conflict in some GM
executions; the reconstructed witness has no such effective later repair. Conditional progress proofs state the
independence, diffusion and coverage assumptions needed for guarantees. They are not certified properties of
the networks.
Supplied scheduling, algebraic predicates and decoding delimit what is learned. Native packages use
heterogeneous protocols, and no architecture ranking follows. Graphs, tapes and first-stage tensors are paired,
17
so decisions and conditions cannot be counted as independent training replicas. Degree transfer changes
palettes and CT preference scale as well; the scale probe does not identify a degree-only effect. Degree
200 and size 4,096 pilots are exploratory and do not define a jointly tested operating region. Output-law
nonrejection is not equality (Appendix J).
10 Conclusion
Local neural policies can achieve broad finite functional coverage and transfer while leaving specific continuation
responses unresolved. Explicit contracts, support audits and component replays make those differences
testable. In these models, better continuation scores sometimes coexist with lower completion because errors
have different reachability and transition consequences. Proposal diversity and scheduled repair provide
further mechanisms by which correct confirmation can coexist with failure. Learning a distributed rule should
therefore be evaluated through declared functional protocols, continuation targets and transfer conditions
together, with each result interpreted at its actual level of evidence.

J Output Laws and Preserved Distribution Studies
For fixed labeled G, let µπ,G be the full terminal-vector/status law including failures/cutoffs. Labels, palette,
interpreter, cutoff, conditioning and color-name quotient must agree before comparing studies. Equal vertex
marginals do not determine a joint law: equally weighted (0, 0)/(1, 1) versus (0, 1)/(1, 0) have equal marginals
and disjoint support. This logical example is not a proper-coloring counterexample on an edge.
Proposition 10 (Symmetry and coupling). On a complete graph with exactly n colors, a vertex-equivariant
stochastic protocol with exchangeable private inputs is uniform over proper labeled colorings conditional on
successful termination of positive probability. Any coupling (X, Y ) satisfies dTV(L(X),L(Y )) ≤ Pr(X ̸= Y ).
Proof. Vertex permutations act transitively on the n! proper coloring bijections; equivariance and the invariant
success event make their conditional masses equal. For any event A, the difference in probabilities of X, Y ∈ A
is at most Pr(X ̸= Y ); take the supremum.
Anonymity alone does not establish the symmetry premises. Untransformed IDs, non-equivariant schedules
and numerical ordering require care. With more than n colors, vertex symmetry need not equate different
color subsets. For r independent paired runs of one graph/policy protocol with zero disagreements, the
one-sided upper limit 1 − α
1/r bounds disagreement and hence TV under that coupling. It says nothing
universal about other graphs/checkpoints. Uniform conditional full-transition coupling error ϵ for H common
pre-divergence steps gives dTV ≤ Hϵ; per-node error would also require a node union bound. Native accuracy
is not this uniform premise.
Global-law, local-palette, robust-statistics and factorial studies remain in their historical directories with
schemas, fixed banks and testing families. Complete runs are observations, permutations act on rows rather
than independently on vertices, and witness selection needs an independent split or valid joint permutation
scheme (Gretton et al., 2012; Hemerik and Goeman, 2018; Phipson and Smyth, 2010; Holm, 1979). Reused
banks are historical reanalysis, not independent new confirmation. Nonrejection does not establish equal
laws or tolerance equivalence; power against localized alternatives matters. These studies support scoped
distributional distinctions, not new MMD/permutation theory, and are outside the 810,000 denominator.
